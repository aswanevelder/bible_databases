## Die bruilof in Kana

**1** En op die derde dag was daar 'n bruilof in Kana van Galilea, en die moeder van Jesus was daar.

**2** En Jesus en sy dissipels is ook na die bruilof genooi.

**3** En toe die wyn opraak, sê die moeder van Jesus vir Hom: "Hulle het nie wyn nie."

**4** En Jesus sê vir haar: "Vrou, wat het Ek met u te doen? My uur het nog nie gekom nie."

**5** Sy moeder sê vir die bediendes: "Wat Hy ook al vir julle sê, doen dit."

**6** En daar het ses klipwaterkruike gestaan, soos die reinigingsgebruik van die Jode vereis, elk met 'n inhoud van twee of drie metrete.

**7** Jesus sê vir hulle: "Maak die kruike vol water." En hulle het hulle tot bo toe volgemaak.

**8** En Hy sê vir hulle: "Skep nou uit en bring dit vir die seremoniemeester." En hulle het dit gebring.

**9** En toe die seremoniemeester die water proe wat wyn geword het — en hy het nie geweet waarvandaan dit was nie, maar die bediendes wat die water geskep het, het geweet — roep die seremoniemeester die bruidegom

**10** en sê vir hom: "Elke mens sit eers die goeie wyn voor, en wanneer die mense dronk geword het, dan die mindere; maar jy het die goeie wyn tot nou toe bewaar."

**11** Hierdie eerste van die tekens het Jesus in Kana van Galilea gedoen, en Hy het sy heerlikheid geopenbaar; en sy dissipels het in Hom geglo.

**12** Daarna het Hy na Kapernaum afgegaan, Hy en sy moeder en sy broers en sy dissipels, en hulle het daar 'n paar dae gebly.

## Jesus reinig die tempel

**13** En die Pasga van die Jode was naby, en Jesus het na Jerusalem opgegaan.

**14** En in die tempel het Hy die mense gevind wat beeste en skape en duiwe verkoop, en die geldwisselaars wat daar sit.

**15** En Hy het 'n sweep van toue gemaak en almal uit die tempel uitgedryf, met die skape en die beeste; en die geld van die wisselaars het Hy uitgegooi en die tafels omgekeer.

**16** En vir die mense wat die duiwe verkoop, het Hy gesê: "Vat hierdie dinge hier weg; moenie die huis van my Vader 'n handelshuis maak nie."

**17** Sy dissipels het onthou dat daar geskrywe staan: "Die ywer vir u huis sal My verteer."

**18** Toe antwoord die Jode en sê vir Hom: "Watter teken toon U aan ons, dat U hierdie dinge doen?"

**19** Jesus antwoord en sê vir hulle: "Breek hierdie tempel af, en in drie dae sal Ek dit oprig."

**20** Toe sê die Jode: "Ses-en-veertig jaar lank is aan hierdie tempel gebou, en U sal dit in drie dae oprig?"

**21** Maar Hy het oor die tempel van sy liggaam gepraat.

**22** Toe Hy dan uit die dode opgewek is, het sy dissipels onthou dat Hy dit gesê het; en hulle het die Skrif geglo en die woord wat Jesus gespreek het.

**23** En terwyl Hy in Jerusalem was op die Pasga, tydens die fees, het baie in sy Naam geglo toe hulle die tekens sien wat Hy gedoen het.

**24** Maar Jesus self het Hom nie aan hulle toevertrou nie, omdat Hy almal geken het,

**25** en omdat Hy nie nodig gehad het dat iemand oor die mens getuig nie, want Hy self het geweet wat in die mens is.

## Translator's notes

**Textual base.** StatResGNT (critical text), now the committed NT base
(`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md` § 2). John 2 has **no significant text-critical variants** affecting the
translation, and none of the open glossary terms (*monogenēs*, *amēn amēn*) occur in this chapter,
so it is a clean first chapter on the new committed base. Editorial markers in the source (`¶`
paragraph marks, `˚` nomina-sacra marks before sacred names) were stripped before translating.

**Verse 1 — "op die derde dag".** *tē hēmera tē tritē* — literal "on the third day", counting from
the events of 1:43ff. Kept literal; the resonance with the resurrection "third day" (cf. v.19) is
the author's, not to be flattened.

**Verse 2 — *mathētai*.** "dissipels" (committed). Consistent throughout (vv.2, 11, 12, 17, 22).

**Verse 4 — *gunai*.** "Vrou" — vocative *gunai* is courteous in Greek (not harsh), but a son
addressing his mother as "Vrou" is striking in both Greek and Afrikaans, and the strangeness is
deliberate in John. Retained literally rather than softened to "Moeder"; worth a footnote.

**Verse 4 — *ti emoi kai soi*.** Literally "what to Me and to you?" — a Semitic idiom marking
distance/disengagement. Rendered "wat het Ek met u te doen?" (the standard Afrikaans idiomatic
equivalent). Note the polite **"u"** for Jesus→Mary throughout, fitting the register.

**Verse 4 — *hē hōra mou*.** "My uur" — a loaded Johannine term (the hour of glorification/cross).
Kept as the bare "uur"; the theological weight is carried by the term itself across the Gospel.

**Verse 6 — *metrētēs*.** Per the measurements policy (preserve source units + footnote), rendered
"metrete" rather than converted to litres (committed at review). **Footnote for print:** a
*metrētēs* ≈ 39 litres, so two-or-three each ≈ 75–115 litres per jar (~450–680 litres total).

**Verse 8 — *architriklinos*.** "seremoniemeester" (committed at review) — the master of the feast
/ head steward who ran the banquet, chosen over "tafelmeester"/"hoofkelner". Rendered consistently
at vv.8, 9.

**Verse 10 — *methysthōsin*.** "dronk geword het" (committed at review). The verb's primary sense
is "have become drunk"; the blunter rendering was chosen over the softer "volop gedrink het" to
stay faithful to the proverb's literal force. The speaker is describing ordinary banquet practice,
not passing moral judgement.

**Verses 11, 23 — *sēmeion*.** "teken" (sign), not "wonder/mirakel". This preserves John's
characteristic theology of *signs* (events that signify who Jesus is). v.11 *archēn tōn sēmeiōn* →
"eerste van die tekens". Proposed as committed glossary term below. *doxa* → "heerlikheid"
(committed).

**Verse 13 — *Pascha*.** "Pasga" (the Jewish Passover), deliberately **not** "Paasfees", which in
Afrikaans usage collides with Easter. Consistent at vv.13, 23. Committed at review as a Gospel-wide
convention.

**Verses 14–16 vs 19–21 — *hieron* / *naos*.** John uses **two** temple words and the distinction
matters here. *hieron* (the whole temple precincts, where trade happened) at vv.14, 15 and *naos*
(the sanctuary proper) at vv.19, 20, 21 are **both** rendered "tempel" per the current glossary,
because Afrikaans has no natural lexical pair. The wordplay — Jesus speaks of the *naos* as His
body — is therefore **flagged for a footnote**. This is exactly the *hieron* 🟡 item in
`GLOSSARY.md`; recommend keeping a single "tempel" rendering + footnote rather than forcing a
distinction like "tempelgebou/heiligdom".

**Verse 17 — OT quotation (Ps 69:9).** "Die ywer vir u huis sal My verteer." **Committed policy**
(`TRANSLATION_POLICY.md` § 2 #8 + § 2a): NT quotations of the OT are rendered **to match our own
OT translation**. Psalm 69 is not yet translated, so this is rendered **provisionally from the
Greek** and entered on the back-harmonisation list in `REVIEW_LOG.md` — to be conformed to our
Afrikaans Ps 69:9 once the Psalms are done. Note for that pass: John follows the LXX of Ps 69:9
(future "sal verteer / *kataphagetai*") against the MT's "has consumed"; preserve our OT wording
in the body and footnote the divergence if the difference is material.

**Verse 22 — *graphē* / *logos*.** "Skrif" and "woord". Here *logos* is the ordinary "statement
Jesus made", **not** the Christological *Logos* of John 1:1 — so lowercase "woord", deliberately
distinct from the capital-W "Woord" reserved for Christ. Noted to prevent false consistency.

**Verses 23–24 — *episteusan* / *episteuen* wordplay.** John puns: many "believed/trusted"
(*episteusan*, v.23) in Him, but Jesus did not "entrust" (*episteuen*, v.24) Himself to them — the
same verb *pisteuō*. Rendered "geglo" (v.23) / "toevertrou" (v.24); the pun is partly lost in
Afrikaans. Noted; a footnote could flag it.

## Self-review report (CLAUDE.md Step 3)

*Pass run against CLAUDE.md and GLOSSARY.md. The translation file above was not altered by this
pass; findings are recorded for the human reviewer.*

1. **Terminology drift** — None within the chapter. *sēmeion* → "teken(s)" consistent (vv.11, 18,
   23); *mathētai* → "dissipels" consistent; *architriklinos* → "seremoniemeester" consistent
   (vv.8, 9); *Pascha* → "Pasga" consistent (vv.13, 23).
2. **Glossary violations** — None. *doxa*→heerlikheid, *patēr*→Vader (cap, of God, v.16),
   *mathētēs*→dissipel all follow committed renderings. *logos* correctly **not** capitalised at
   v.22 (ordinary word, not the Christological Logos).
3. **Sentence-level consistency** — The *hieron* (vv.14–15) / *naos* (vv.19–21) pair is rendered
   uniformly as "tempel" by deliberate decision (flagged, not drift). The repeated "die goeie wyn"
   (v.10) and "in drie dae … oprig" (vv.19–20) are rendered identically across speaker turns.
4. **Awkward Afrikaans** — "metrete" (v.6) is opaque without the footnote; flagged. "seremoniemeester"
   is long but clear. Otherwise the register reads naturally and matches john-01.
5. **Untranslated theological/literary loading** — Flagged for footnotes: the *hieron/naos*
   body-as-temple wordplay (vv.19–21), the *episteusan/episteuen* pun (vv.23–24), *gunai* as
   address (v.4), and "My uur" (v.4). One open **policy** item surfaced: OT-in-NT quotation
   handling (v.17, Ps 69:9) — first occurrence; needs a team decision.

**Recommendation:** ready for human review. The chapter introduces several conventions that set
Gospel-wide precedent (*sēmeion*→teken, *Pascha*→Pasga, *hieron/naos*→tempel+footnote) — the team
should confirm these, and commit the OT-quotation policy, before large-scale Gospel work.
