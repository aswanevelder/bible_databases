# PROPER_NAMES.md — Committed proper-name renderings

> The registry of committed Afrikaans forms for biblical proper names (people and places).
> Genealogies generate many names, so they get their own file rather than crowding `GLOSSARY.md`.
> This file is the **source of truth** for name forms; `GLOSSARY.md` covers common vocabulary.

---

## Governing policy (COMMITTED 2026-05-31)

**Render from the Hebrew/Greek, not from the Afrikaans tradition — check the source for every name.**

1. **Identical source name → identical Afrikaans rendering.** If the Hebrew/Greek is the same
   (even for two *different people*), render it the same. Do **not** disambiguate by spelling.
2. **Different-but-similar source names → distinct Afrikaans renderings.** Preserve the distinction
   the source makes, even when the names sound alike.
3. Where the source **deliberately uses identical or similar names to create literary parallelism**
   (as the Cainite and Sethite genealogies do), the translation **must preserve that parallelism,
   even at the cost of reader confusion.** **Footnotes disambiguate; spelling does not.**
4. Established Afrikaans forms are used for well-known names (Moses, Abraham, Jerusalem); lesser
   names are transliterated with standard Afrikaans phonology. AFR53 may be consulted for the
   *form* only (a fact about Afrikaans usage), never for surrounding phrasing.

**Status:** ✅ committed · 🟡 provisional · 🔴 open

## The Genesis 4–5 parallel genealogies

The Cainite line (Gen 4:17–24) and the Sethite line (Gen 5) echo each other by design. Some names
are **identical** in Hebrew (same person-name reused for different people); others are **similar but
distinct**. The translation preserves both relationships; footnotes carry the disambiguation
(see Gen 5:21 and 5:25 footnotes).

| Cainite line (Gen 4) | Hebrew | Sethite line (Gen 5) | Hebrew | Relationship |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Kain** | קַיִן *Qayin* | **Kenan** | קֵינָן *Qenan* | similar → **distinct** |
| **Henog** | חֲנוֹךְ *Chanokh* | **Henog** | חֲנוֹךְ *Chanokh* | **identical** → identical |
| **Irad** | עִירָד *ʿIrad* | **Jered** | יֶרֶד *Yered* | similar → **distinct** |
| **Mehújael** | מְחוּיָאֵל *Mechuya'el* | **Mahalálel** | מַהֲלַלְאֵל *Mahalal'el* | similar → **distinct** |
| **Metúsael** | מְתוּשָׁאֵל *Methusha'el* | **Metúsalag** | מְתוּשֶׁלַח *Methushelach* | similar → **distinct** |
| **Lameg** | לֶמֶךְ *Lemech* | **Lameg** | לֶמֶךְ *Lemech* | **identical** → identical |

Detailed entries for the four most-confusable names:

**חֲנוֹךְ *Chanokh* → Henog ✅** — Two distinct people share this name: (a) Gen 4:17, Cainite line,
son of Cain, a city named after him; (b) Gen 5:18–24, Sethite line, "walked with God and was not".
Render identically; do not disambiguate by spelling. Translator's note/footnote at Gen 5:25 covers
the parallel.

**לֶמֶךְ *Lemech* → Lameg ✅** — Two distinct people: (a) Gen 4:18–24, Cainite line, polygamist and
violent boaster; (b) Gen 5:25–31, Sethite line, father of Noah. Render identically; do not
disambiguate by spelling. Footnote required at Gen 5:25 explaining the parallel (present).

**מְתוּשָׁאֵל *Methusha'el* → Metúsael ✅** — Gen 4:18, Cainite line. Distinct from Metúsalag
(Gen 5:21, Sethite line): phonetically similar but the Hebrew differs (*-sha'el* vs *-shelach*).
The author uses similar-sounding names across the two lines as deliberate parallelism.

**מְתוּשֶׁלַח *Methushelach* → Metúsalag ✅** — Gen 5:21–27, Sethite line; father of Lameg,
grandfather of Noah. Distinct from Metúsael (Gen 4:18, Cainite line). Footnote at Gen 5:21 (present).
"Metúsalag" is the familiar Afrikaans form.
